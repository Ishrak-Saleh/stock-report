Who adds tracking

Decision: the supplier is the main source, and staff can always enter a number for them. Both come straight from the SOP, so no permission changes. What changes is how it reads and flows.

What the SOP says

Quoted from sections 2 to 4
The supplier must be able to see their orders, confirm them, and add tracking.Section 2
Add one or more tracking numbers per order, each with its unit count. The units on tracking can't add up to more than the order. The user may finish entering tracking later.Section 3, a must. It says "the user", so staff are included.
Staff see and manage everything.Section 3, Access, a must
Ask for tracking: Confirmed 10 or more days ago, with units still without tracking.Section 4, chase rules. The message goes to the supplier, so the supplier is the expected source.

What we do with it

Behavior and wording
  • The supplier path is the short one: Confirm, then Add tracking, with the units already filled in.
  • Staff add a number when it arrives by email or phone. The dialog says who they are entering it for, and the history reads "Sam for Acme".
  • Only staff can remove a tracking number.
  • A tracking number also counts as a confirmation, as the SOP's stage rule says, so staff do not need a separate step.
  • A part-tracked order is normal. After saving, the page says how many units are left and offers Add another shipment.

Decisions and how they fit the system

Why this layout

  • Suppliers get a smaller app, not a locked copy of the big one. Fewer tabs means fewer wrong taps.
  • The headline puts the thing they owe us first: confirm, then tracking.
  • The "can and cannot" panel is on the page once so a supplier never wonders why Edit is missing.
  • Errors keep the top bar. A page that is not yours says "not found", which does not reveal that it exists. Staff-only pages tell a supplier plainly that they are not allowed.

How it connects to the code

  • User has a role and, for staff, a company. A supplier has no company: it is matched to Supplier rows by email. The signed session cookie holds a small user record, and the key is read from the environment, never the repo.
  • Two FastAPI dependencies, require_user and require_staff, guard routers. One rule in app/tenancy.py then filters every database query by the signed-in company or supplier email, so a page cannot forget to filter and hiding a button is never the only protection.
  • There are no CSRF tokens. The cookie is SameSite Lax and every POST must come from the same site (an Origin check). Responses add security headers and no-store caching, and passwords are hashed with scrypt.
  • OrderEvent.by becomes the signed-in person's name, which fixes the placeholder name in history.

Rules and honest limits

  • The supplier's Confirm and Add tracking write the same events as staff do, labeled with the supplier's name. Staff acting for a supplier are labeled "Sam for Acme".
  • Tracking units cannot exceed the order total, checked in domain/orders.py.
  • Login throttling is simple and in-memory, and a sign-up limit of 10 accounts an hour per address works the same way. Good for a demo, and the design note says so.
  • There is no email check at sign up. Anyone who knows a supplier's address could sign up as that supplier and see its orders. A real product would send a confirmation link first.
  • The supplier cannot see forecasts. That is a deliberate choice, since forecasts hold the buyer's own planning.
